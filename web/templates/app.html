Barbs are drawn for the lowest aircraft on the approach; tap an aircraft (on the profile or the map) or its strip to follow another one. The map keeps the place and zoom you leave it at; ⌂ goes back to the airport. Departures (climb-outs) are hidden unless you choose to show them; they are marked with their runway and ↑. A speed vector shows where the aircraft will be after 30 s (1 or 2 min) at its ground speed. Altitudes are shown as on a controller's radar label: A023 = 2 300 ft on the QNH, FL061 above 5 000 ft. Aircraft type on the map adds the ICAO type after the callsign (FIN456 AT75). With Selected altitude on, the altitude set on the aircraft's autopilot panel (MCP / FCU) follows when the aircraft sends it: A023+A050 = at 2 300 ft, climbing (+), 5 000 ft selected; FL120-A030 = descending (−) to 3 000 ft; = level; FL above the transition altitude. With either of them on, the map's labels and squares are a little smaller. Labels on the map: ATC gives every aircraft the ATC style's three lines in a small font — callsign / level, ↑ ↓ and selected level in hundreds of feet (007↓020) / ground speed in tens of knots and type (14 A320). A landed aircraft fades 15 s after its last position and goes after 30 s. The chart under the view shows the windshear of every landing and departure with their turbulence (WS + EDR, the default) or their approach conditions (WS + ACI) for the last 3, 6 or 12 h. Turbulence on the map (research): the straight pieces of the landings' and departures' paths of the last 30 min or 1 h coloured by their EDR — green smooth, yellow light (0.10), orange moderate (0.20), red severe (0.45); the strongest on top; a line under the aircraft gives the maximum and the median below and above 1000 ft. The boundary-layer line shows the low-level jet (LLJ) and a surface inversion (INV) of the last hour, when there is one. The approach history under the strips lists the landings of the last 3 or 6 h (of the runways chosen) as in the History view, by default with their turbulence; tap a landing for all its bands, its reports and the boundary layer at the time.

ACI + EDR + WS (research): between the ACI and the windshear the turbulence (EDR) of each landing (dot) and departure (ring) from its own Mode S replies, with the ICAO lines light 0.10, moderate 0.20 and severe 0.45; tap a flight to mark it in all three. Boundary layer (research): the wind (or the temperature against the surface) from 0 to 5000 ft hour by hour, the low-level jet (◆ its nose and speed, joined hour to hour) and the surface inversion (red bar along the bottom); hatched where there were no flights. Space–time: the headwind change of every height band of the landings and departures against the flights before them — blue less, red more. A front is a streak through the flights. Tap a cell for its flights.

The MEPS model of MET Norway (CC BY 4.0), the newest run, from 6 h back to 24 or 48 h ahead, hour by hour, over Finland and the Baltic Sea. Rain and snow of the hour before the shown time. ▶ plays the loaded hours; tap the map for the values of a place (they follow the slider), or an aircraft for its details. Data per hour at 10 km: about 20 kB with rain and isobars, about 50 kB with wind and gusts, about 70 kB with clouds too — 54 hours with rain and isobars about 1 MB (20 km: about a third). "Near the slider" loads only ±6 h around it. Air traffic: the aircraft the receiver hears now, updated every 6 s (about 1–3 MB an hour), whatever hour the slider shows; the ATC map style and labels as on the Live Map — a research display, not for air traffic control.
