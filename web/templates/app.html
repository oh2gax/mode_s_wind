Barbs are drawn for the lowest aircraft on the approach; tap an aircraft (on the profile or the map) or its strip to follow another one. The map keeps the place and zoom you leave it at; ⌂ goes back to the airport. Departures (climb-outs) are hidden unless you choose to show them; they are marked with their runway and ↑. A speed vector shows where the aircraft will be after 30 s (1 or 2 min) at its ground speed. Altitudes are shown as on a controller's radar label: A023 = 2 300 ft on the QNH, FL061 above 5 000 ft. Aircraft type on the map adds the ICAO type after the callsign (FIN456 AT75). With Selected altitude on, the altitude set on the aircraft's autopilot panel (MCP / FCU) follows when the aircraft sends it: A023+A050 = at 2 300 ft, climbing (+), 5 000 ft selected; FL120-A030 = descending (−) to 3 000 ft; = level; FL above the transition altitude. With either of them on, the map's labels and squares are a little smaller. Labels on the map: ATC gives every aircraft the ATC style's three lines in a small font — callsign / level, ↑ ↓ and selected level in hundreds of feet (007↓020) / ground speed in tens of knots and type (14 A320). A landed aircraft fades 15 s after its last position and goes after 30 s. The chart under the view shows the windshear of every landing and departure with their turbulence (WS + EDR, the default) or their approach conditions (WS + ACI) for the last 3, 6 or 12 h. Boundary layer below (research; Off by default) adds one of the boundary-layer charts under it on the same hours — the wind or the temperature hour by hour, or the headwind, its change or the shear flight by flight — also with the chart off; tap it for a cell or a flight. Turbulence on the map (research): the straight pieces of the landings' and departures' paths of the last 30 min or 1 h coloured by their EDR — green smooth, yellow light (0.10), orange moderate (0.20), red severe (0.45); the strongest on top; a line under the aircraft gives the maximum and the median below and above 1000 ft. The boundary-layer line shows the low-level jet (LLJ) and a surface inversion (INV) of the last hour, when there is one. The approach history under the strips lists the landings of the last 3 or 6 h (of the runways chosen) as in the History view, by default with their turbulence; with Departures on the map: Shown also the departures, in one list with the landings (their runway in a light-blue box), as the History view's List: Both; tap a flight for all its bands, its reports and the boundary layer at the time.

ACI + EDR + WS (research): between the ACI and the windshear the turbulence (EDR) of each landing (dot) and departure (ring) from its own Mode S replies, with the ICAO lines light 0.10, moderate 0.20 and severe 0.45; tap a flight to mark it in all three. EDR + WS: the same without the ACI. Boundary layer below (with ACI only, ACI + windshear, ACI + EDR + WS and EDR + WS): one of the boundary-layer charts under the chart, on the same hours, with its own tap read-out — e.g. EDR + WS with the BL headwind of every flight. Boundary layer (research): the wind (or the temperature against the surface) from 0 to 5000 ft hour by hour, the low-level jet (◆ its nose and speed, joined hour to hour) and the surface inversion (red bar along the bottom); hatched where there were no flights. BL headwind, BL Δ headwind and BL shear (research): the same flight by flight — every landing and departure a column with its headwind, its change against the flights of the previous 45 min, or the wind shear of its own profile; tap a column for the flight. Space–time: the headwind change of every height band of the landings and departures against the flights before them — blue less, red more. A front is a streak through the flights. Tap a cell for its flights.
Mast options
Range
Chart top
EFHK now box
The Kivenlahti mast (FMI, Espoo, 24 km south-west of the airport) measures the temperature at 2–297 m and the wind at 26–327 m every 10 minutes. EFHK now: the airport's model weather, its difference to the METAR and the mast's values against the model (Δ MEPS) and the aircraft (Δ Mode S); tap a row for what it means, « for one line. The boundary-layer charts show the mast's 10-minute values below the amber line (its top, about 1 200 ft) and the aircraft's hourly values above it (Mast in the charts: Fill; Columns — the mast's hour means as a narrow column at the right of each hour, beside the aircraft's cells, which can then be seen below 1 200 ft too; Off — the aircraft only) — the temperature against the METAR (red warmer: an inversion), or the wind speed with barbs; the strip between them is the mast's temperature difference top − 2 m (violet: an inversion). Tap a chart for the values; a tap on the mast also shows its profile of that time (Now × returns to the newest). The time series shows the mast's winds and temperatures (the temperature axis inverted, colder up) and, as hollow circles, the airport's Mode S value of each hour at the same height above the ground (Top: the 327 m wind and 297 m temperature; All: 92–327 m) with the METAR (□); Mast 1 h shows the mast as a running 1-hour mean, closer to the hourly Mode S values; Mast Off leaves only the Mode S hours. The comparison gives the mean difference and the RMS of the aircraft (Mode S), the model (MEPS) and the soundings against the mast at the same height above the ground.

The MEPS model of MET Norway (CC BY 4.0), the newest run, from 6 h back to 24 or 48 h ahead, hour by hour, over Finland and the Baltic Sea. Rain and snow of the hour before the shown time. Temperature (2 m): Colours — bands of 2 °C, blue below freezing, cyan at 0 °C, then green, yellow, orange and red, with the 0 °C line; Isotherms — lines every 2 °C (4 °C zoomed out), 0 °C thick; a tap gives the value. Temperature numbers (on its own, also with Temperature Off): the whole degrees in one colour over the whole map, inland and at sea; Temperature numbers grid: Normal (about 2 cm apart), Dense (the default, about 1.5 cm) or Very dense (about 1 cm) — inside the 2.5 km detail box (zoomed in) the numbers are its own values on its own grid, so they can come as close as its cells. Stored from the runs of 1 Nov 2026 on. Rain source: the FMI radar (FMI open data, CC BY 4.0) is the newest composite — the rain rate of the last 5–15 minutes, about 2 km, Finland and the nearby seas — so it is shown only at the present hour (Now); the other hours show the model. FMI radar: the radar alone at Now; Both: the radar inside its coverage (dashed edge) and the model's rain outside it and as outlines inside it. About 10–150 kB every 4 minutes, depending on the rain. Opacity sets how strongly all the filled layers are drawn — rain and snow, the radar, gusts, clouds, fog and the temperature colours (75 % the default; isobars, barbs, lines and numbers stay as they are); with Both the model's rain outside the radar area is drawn a third fainter than the radar, so the radar stands out. Detail near EFHK: zoomed in (zoom 7 and closer) the model's full 2.5 km grid in a 300 × 300 km box around the airport (faint dotted edge) — showers, fog patches and the coast's effect sharper; about 8 kB per hour more with rain and isobars, about 30 kB with wind, gusts and clouds, only while zoomed in. Shading: Smooth (the default) draws the values interpolated between the grid points — rain, snow, the radar and gusts as smooth contour bands, clouds and fog as smooth shading, with the same values and colour steps; Cells each grid cell in one colour, as the model stores it. ▶ plays the loaded hours; tap the map for the values of a place (they follow the slider), or an aircraft for its details. Data per hour at 10 km: about 20 kB with rain and isobars, about 50 kB with wind and gusts, about 70 kB with clouds too — 54 hours with rain and isobars about 1 MB (20 km: about a third). "Near the slider" loads only ±6 h around it. Air traffic: the aircraft the receiver hears now, updated every 6 s (about 1–3 MB an hour), whatever hour the slider shows; the ATC map style and labels as on the Live Map — a research display, not for air traffic control. Receivers: the receivers as small triangles at their positions (to about 1 km; with more receivers each in its colour, RX-01 blue, RX-02 violet) — tap one for its name.

Zone: the distance from the receiver (as the GPS Quality page). Window: the chart and the spoofing indicators — 24 h to 7 d hour by hour, 1 m (31 days) and 3 m (90 days) day by day as aircraft-hours (the spoofing indicators at most the last 31 days); the tiles are always the last 24 h. Severe: the position is unusable (NACp 0, NIC 0 or no own position). Spoofing indicators: the own ADS-B position against MLAT, the GNSS − barometric altitude difference and position jumps — two or more independent ones (2+) are much stronger evidence than one. The full GPS Quality and Episodes pages are on the full site. Remembered on the phone.
