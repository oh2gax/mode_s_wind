What is MODE-S Wind?

An upper-air observing system built on aircraft as sensors. A single Mode S / ADS-B receiver (Jetvision Radarcape) near Helsinki-Vantaa (EFHK) records the transponder replies that the ground radars and Fintraffic's wide-area multilateration (WAM) network interrogate from every aircraft — Mode S Enhanced Surveillance registers BDS 5,0 (true track, ground speed, true airspeed, roll) and BDS 6,0 (magnetic heading, IAS, Mach), BDS 4,4 meteorological reports where available — and the ADS-B broadcasts (position, 2-Hz velocity, GNSS − barometric height difference). From these it derives wind and temperature profiles from the surface to about FL400, several thousand observations an hour within a few hundred kilometres, continuously, on a Raspberry Pi 4.

The observations themselves need no model input. A few small calibrations are learnt continuously and stored locally: the per-airframe heading offsets from the fleet itself (each aircraft against the others), the GNSS heights of the pressure surfaces against the Jokioinen soundings, and the upper-level temperature bias and the humidity-trial offsets against the MEPS model (or the soundings). If the soundings or the model are not available, the system keeps running on the last learnt calibrations, and without any of them on the aircraft data alone.

How the observations are made

  • Wind — the ground-velocity vector (BDS 5,0) minus the air-velocity vector (TAS along the true heading: BDS 6,0 magnetic heading + WMM2025 declination + a per-airframe heading calibration).
  • Temperature — static air temperature from the Mach number and the true airspeed, T = TAS² / (γR·M²); a small upper-level warm bias (≈ +0.7 K at 300–200 hPa) is corrected.
  • Heights of the pressure surfaces — pressure altitude + the GNSS − baro difference of ADS-B, calibrated against the radiosondes: D-values, geopotential height tendencies and layer thickness.
  • Verification — every Jokioinen sounding and every MEPS hour is compared automatically. Typical numbers: wind 3–4 kt vector RMS against MEPS at 925–500 hPa, temperature about 1 K RMS against the sondes, pressure-surface heights within 3–10 m of MEPS (hourly correlation 0.996–0.998).

Derived products

  • Soundings — Skew-T log-p of the area (now and an hourly archive) and of single climbs / descents, with an automatic analysis — surface-parcel LCL, LFC, CAPE / CIN and Lifted Index (METAR dewpoint), inversions, Richardson-number turbulence layers, thermal advection from the veering / backing wind, low-level jet, tropopause, freezing level and Schmidt–Appleman contrail levels — side by side with the radiosonde and the MEPS model.
  • Time–height sections over the airport — temperature anomaly, 3-h temperature change (advection), wind speed, lapse rate with inversions and the tropopause, jet-core height, vertical motion (adiabatic method, cm/s, FL050–FL200), relative vorticity (plane fit of the aircraft winds over 200 km), D-values and 6-h height change, and a thickness-temperature check (virtual temperature from GNSS heights against the Mach/TAS temperature).
  • Constant-level analyses (Wind Map) — gridded wind and temperature at any flight level, isotachs and streamlines, jet axis, temperature anomaly (fronts, air masses), height contours with the geostrophic wind, and differences against an earlier period, another level or the MEPS model.
  • Boundary layer at the airport — every landing and take-off is a 200-ft-resolution profile of the lowest 3 000 ft (wind, headwind, temperature): low-level jets, night inversions, frontal shear.
  • Humidity (trial) — moisture from the virtual-temperature excess Tv − T of the thickness, the principle of the ADS-B humidity study at Amsterdam Schiphol; still being evaluated.

Windshear detection v2

Runs on the server for every aircraft on final at EFHK, whether the page is open or not. Along each approach path the headwind (TAS − GS, straight from the aircraft's replies, every 2–3 s) is searched for ramps of 0.4–4 km, scaled to a 1-km equivalent and tested against the replies' own noise, after the ramp method of the Hong Kong Observatory's LIDAR windshear alerting (Shun & Chan 2008; Hon & Chan 2021); the F-factor (Proctor, Hinton & Bowles 2000) is computed over 1 km. A second, independent check compares each landing's and departure's wind vector in 200-ft bands with the median of all landings and departures of the previous 45 minutes, catching sudden wind changes between flights (gust fronts, frontal passages). Alert levels follow the airport alerting practice (Cole & Todd 1996): WS advisory 10 kt, windshear alert 15 kt (loss or gain), microburst alert 30 kt headwind loss. Every landing and departure gets a stored windshear report. Experimental research output.

Why it is interesting

  • Independent of models — everything is measured by the aircraft; the MEPS model and the radiosondes are optional, for verification and two small calibrations, and the system falls back to the aircraft alone without them.
  • Resolution — an updated profile every few minutes right over the airport, where the nearest radiosonde (Jokioinen, about 100 km away) gives three a day; dense sampling of the boundary layer on final and climb-out.
  • Open and reproducible — commodity hardware, open data (FMI, MET Norway), free software (GPLv3); the hourly products and the raw reply series of every approach are stored for re-analysis.

Special credits

MODE-S Wind stands on the work of two researchers in the Netherlands.

Siebren de Haan (KNMI, the Royal Netherlands Meteorological Institute) showed that wind and temperature derived from Mode S EHS radar replies are accurate and dense enough for meteorology, and brought them into numerical weather prediction. The author's own interest in the subject began in 2009 with de Haan's KNMI report below; his first ADS-B receiver had been running since 2006, when all of this was still new.

  • de Haan, S., 2009: Quality assessment of high resolution wind and temperature observation from ModeS. KNMI Scientific Report WR 2009-07 (revised 2010), De Bilt. KNMI
  • de Haan, S., 2011: High-resolution wind and temperature observations from aircraft tracked by Mode-S air traffic control radar. J. Geophys. Res., 116, D10111. doi:10.1029/2010JD015264
  • de Haan, S. and A. Stoffelen, 2012: Assimilation of high-resolution Mode-S wind and temperature observations in a regional NWP model. Wea. Forecasting, 27, 918–937. doi:10.1175/WAF-D-11-00088.1

Junzi Sun (TU Delft, Faculty of Aerospace Engineering) wrote pyModeS, the open-source Mode S / ADS-B decoder that this system is built on, the open book that explains the signals, and research on reconstructing the weather from aircraft surveillance data.

  • Sun, J., H. Vû, J. Ellerbroek and J. M. Hoekstra, 2020: pyModeS: Decoding Mode-S surveillance data for open air transportation research. IEEE Trans. Intell. Transp. Syst., 21(7), 2777–2786. doi:10.1109/TITS.2019.2914770 · github.com/junzis/pyModeS
  • Sun, J., 2021: The 1090 Megahertz Riddle: A Guide to Decoding Mode S and ADS-B Signals. TU Delft OPEN Books. doi:10.34641/mg.11
  • Sun, J., H. Vû, J. Ellerbroek and J. M. Hoekstra, 2018: Weather field reconstruction using aircraft surveillance data and a novel meteo-particle model. PLOS ONE, 13(10), e0205029. doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0205029
