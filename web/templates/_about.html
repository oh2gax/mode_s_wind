What is MODE-S Wind?

MODE-S Wind turns the radio replies of the aircraft around Helsinki-Vantaa into weather observations. When the ground radars and the wide-area multilateration (WAM) network interrogate an aircraft, its transponder answers with its true airspeed, heading, ground speed, track and Mach number (Mode S Enhanced Surveillance), and broadcasts its position and velocity (ADS-B). From these the system computes the wind and the air temperature at every aircraft, every few seconds, from the runway up to 40 000 ft — thousands of observations an hour from one receiver and a Raspberry Pi 4.

Main features

  • Live Map — every aircraft with its wind and temperature, and an always-on atmosphere profile.
  • Sounding — Skew-T soundings from the aircraft (now, hourly archive, single flights) with stability indices, compared with the Jokioinen radiosonde and the MEPS weather model; time–height section.
  • Wind Map — wind and temperature at any flight level over the whole coverage, the jet stream and the heights of the pressure surfaces from the aircraft's GNSS altitudes.
  • Windshear — all approaches and departures at EFHK, with windshear detection running on the server for every flight and a report for every landing.
  • Weather — the MEPS model map: isobars, rain and snow, 10 m wind and gusts, clouds.
  • GPS Quality and GPS Episodes — monitoring of the GNSS interference in the area.

Why it is different

  • Measured, not modelled — everything comes from the aircraft themselves; no model data is needed. The model and the radiosondes are optional, for comparison and small calibrations.
  • Dense in time and space — an up-to-date profile every few minutes right over the airport, where a radiosonde gives one a few times a day, 100 km away.
  • Low-level detail — every landing and take-off is a small sounding of the lowest 3 000 ft, where wind shear and inversions matter most.
  • Low cost and open — an off-the-shelf receiver, a Raspberry Pi and open data; free software (GPLv3).
