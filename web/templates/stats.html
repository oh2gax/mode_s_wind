MLAT · pair recorder
Two receivers with GPS time stamp the same reply at their own antennas. The difference of the two stamps is the raw material of multilateration: 1 ns is 0.3 m of range difference. The recorder keeps every such pair (raw files by the hour) and a summary per aircraft; it only records, and nothing of it goes into the weather. Two receivers give one time difference per reply, which is not yet a position.

Measured − expected, last 10 min (pairs per 25 ns; the label is the lower edge)
Median by direction from the middle of the two sites (ns)
For an aircraft with a fresh own ADS-B position the expected time difference follows from the distances to the two sites; the table shows measured − expected. The median is the constant part (cable and receiver delays, errors of the site positions), the spread is the timing noise that limits the accuracy of a later position. A first, rough value: the aircraft's height is its barometric altitude, corrected with its own GPS height difference where it sends one, and the sites are the receivers' GPS antennas unless the 1090 MHz antenna positions are given in the settings. The values of a window are the median of its minutes and the typical range within a minute; the line shows the last 24 h by the minute. The histogram shows the shape of the scatter. The direction chart shows whether the median depends on where the aircraft is: an error in a site position gives a pattern there, a cable or receiver delay does not.
