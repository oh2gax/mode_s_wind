{% endmacro %} {% macro sensors(fold=false) %}
Aircraft as sensors
{% if fold %}
About this panel{% endif %}
Every aircraft that sends its heading and speeds is a wind sensor, and not all are equally good. One dot is one airframe (no identities are shown), one row an aircraft type — the ten most common and, in amber below them, the propeller types; the line is the type's median. Heading offset: what the system has learnt to take off the airframe's reported heading so that its winds agree with the other aircraft (airframes beyond the limit are left out of the winds). Direction-dependent heading error: the part of the heading error that changes once around the compass — large on aircraft that sense their heading magnetically (turboprops such as the ATR), near zero where it comes from an inertial platform (the jets); given only when the headings an airframe has flown determine it. Flight-to-flight spread: how much an airframe's offset differs from one flight to the next; a fixed correction cannot remove that. Wind difference from the others: the vector difference of its winds from the other aircraft in the same layer and hour, after its heading correction (it includes the real differences of the wind within the area). The last three are collected from 10 Nov 2026 on and fill up over the following days. GNSS gate: winds of the ATR 72-600 and PC-12 withheld since the server started because the aircraft reported a degraded GNSS position (their ground speed is then dead-reckoned).

{% endmacro %} {% macro cad(fold=false) %}
Reply cadence per aircraft (WAM / radar)
{% if fold %}
About this panel{% endif %}
Time between successive replies of the same aircraft as heard here. Final: below 5 000 ft within 25 NM of EFHK; terminal: below 10 000 ft within 60 NM; en route: the rest. Replies closer than 0.5 s (ADS-B squitters: 0.15 s) are counted as near-duplicates (the same register answered twice, e.g. to two interrogators), gaps over 60 s are not intervals. The receiver hears the aircraft's replies (1090 MHz), not the interrogations (1030 MHz): a longer interval can also be a reply that did not reach this receiver.
