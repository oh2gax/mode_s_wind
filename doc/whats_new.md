# What's new in MODE-S Wind

Short notes for users of the web pages, newest first. Each version is one day of
development (v.DDMMYYYY). The full technical history is in CHANGELOG.md.

## v.28102026 — History, space–time, Statistics and an approach map on the phone; a better search; maps remember their view; Pi or Server; signal chart

- **MODE-S Wind on your phone (`/app`): History** — a new tile shows the landings or the departures of the last hour to a day, or of any date, as cards: time, flight, type, runway, the ACI and windshear level, and a strip with the headwind (or crosswind, or wind) in each height band. The runway is shown in a small frame, lined up from card to card. Tap a card for all its bands and its reports. The search field on top finds a callsign, registration or aircraft type, e.g. *FIN7*, *OH-LX* or *AT75*.
- **Space–time on the phone**: the *ACI · WS · ST* tile (until now *ACI & WS*) has a third choice, **Space–time** — the same diagram as on the Windshear page, showing how the wind changes move through the successive flights. Tap a cell for its flights.
- **ACI · WS · ST — the list under the chart follows your choice**: it now lists the flights of the chosen period (before, always the latest 15 landings) — with *ACI + windshear* the departures too, with *Space–time* the flights of the diagram, or of the cell you tap. 15 rows, **Show all** for the rest, and **Landings / Departures in History** open the History view with the same period.
- **Approach History search** on the Windshear page: the box now finds a callsign, registration, aircraft type or ICAO address anywhere in the text (before only the beginning of a registration).
- Lighter on mobile data: the landings list now comes compressed and, for the phone, without the details it does not show.
- **A map in the phone's ILS approach view**: the aircraft on the approach are now also shown on a map above the glideslope profile (beside it when the phone is turned), as on the Windshear page — callsign and altitude (easy to read in both themes), the ILS lines and the airport. Tap an aircraft on the map to see its wind barbs on the profile. In the menu, *Show* chooses Profile, Map or Both, and *Aircraft on the map* also the other aircraft near the airport. The map stays where you leave it; the house button takes you back to the airport.
- **Statistics on the phone**: a new **Statistics** tile in `/app` shows what the receiver hears — the message rate (and the data transfer), the downlink formats, the meteo yield and the range rings, for the last 24 hours (or 10 min, 1 h, 7 d). In the menu's *Statistics options* you choose which sections are shown: signal strength, Comm-B registers, ADS-B type codes, reply cadence, the Radarcape receiver, the interrogators and the system health can be switched on. Each panel's explanation is folded under *About this panel*; tap the range rings for the farthest aircraft in that direction.
- **The time in UTC on the phone**: all times in MODE-S Wind are UTC (two hours behind Finnish time in winter), so `/app` now shows the current UTC time at the top right.
- **Light, dark or automatic on the phone**: the theme button at the top right of `/app` has three settings — **Dark** (moon), **Light** (sun) and **Auto** (half circle), which follows your phone's own dark / light setting, also when it changes in the evening. Each tap steps to the next one. Auto is used until you choose one.
- **About** now also tells about the two sites: the cloud server that serves you, and the Raspberry Pi station at the receiver that keeps observing even without the internet.
- **About and all the user guides on the phone**: the menu's *General* section now has **About** (the same as on the full site) and **User guides** — every guide, e.g. the Windshear guide or the windshear study log, opens as PDF on the phone.
- The *MODE-S Wind on a phone* user guide (version 1.9) has the new views.
- **Windshear study log (version 1.3)**: the first night of the trial ramps along the climb-outs — a night-time low-level jet over Helsinki-Vantaa, which the departures climbed straight into, and what that means before the departure ramps may give alerts.
- **Live Map, Wind Map and Weather remember where you were**: the map now opens with the same zoom and position you left it at. The new **⌂** button under the + / − buttons takes you back to the receiver.
- **Statistics page — Pi or Server**: the last health dot shows the computer the page runs on. On the cloud server it is now called **Server** (it never showed the receiving station's Pi there); on the Raspberry Pi it is still **Pi**. The Statistics user guide (version 1.7) explains it.
- **Statistics page — signal strength chart**: the chart now spans only the levels the receiver actually reports (here about −28 to 0 dBFS instead of −50 to 0), so the bars fill the chart and the scale is readable. The Statistics user guide (version 1.8) has the new picture.

## v.27102026 — Windshear alerts confirmed or isolated; departures compared fairly

- **Statistics page: data transfer** — a new tab **Data transfer** beside *Message rate* shows how much data the server moves: what it receives from the receiver (the raw Beast stream, the aircraft list), what it sends to the users, and everything through its network connection — now, today, yesterday, the last 7 days and this month, with an estimate for the whole month, and as a chart per minute, hour or day. The Statistics user guide (version 1.5) explains it.
- **Windshear page — confirmed or isolated**: a shear layer or a front is met by every aircraft that passes it, a turbulent eddy in strong wind by just one. Each ramp alert now shows whether the other landings on the same runway within 15 minutes saw the same: **✓2** = seen by two of them, **iso** = by none (probably turbulence), **iso?** = the landings after it are not known yet. Shown in the alert log and the Approach History *WS* view (hover for the details); the alert level itself does not change.
- **Fairer comparison of departures**: the aircraft's airspeed reads a little differently on approach and in the climb — departures showed about 3 kt more headwind than the landings at the same height, which made many departures look like a windshear advisory with "more headwind". Departures are now compared with the other departures (and landings with landings), or the difference is removed first. Fewer false departure advisories.
- The Windshear user guide (version 1.8) explains both, with the turbulent southerly day of 26 October as an example.
- **New: the windshear study log** (in the *User guides* tab) — how the windshear detection has developed and why: from the six first algorithms to the Hong Kong Observatory's method and each change since, with the checks of the collected data. Written for meteorologists and scientists, and kept up to date as the study goes on.
- **The receiving station** overview (in the *User guides* tab): three A4 pages with photos of the antenna, receiver and workstation, how the aircraft's replies become wind and temperature, the calibrations and the pages — and now also a page on the windshear detection and the ongoing study, and one on the two sites: the station at the receiver and the new cloud server.
- **New address: https://mode-s-wind.oh2gax.org** — MODE-S Wind now runs on a cloud server with a secure address; on a phone use **https://mode-s-wind.oh2gax.org/app** (and *Add to Home Screen* again from there). Please update your bookmarks and log in there with your personal user name and password. The old address shows a notice with the new one, and **Open this page there** takes you to the same page.
- **Windshear — space–time diagram**: a new **ST** button beside WR / ACI / WS shows how the wind changes move through the successive landings and departures — height upwards, time to the right, blue where the headwind dropped compared with the flights before, red where it rose. A front shows as a coloured streak through the flights, turbulence as scattered single cells. Hover a cell for its flights.
- **Windshear — departures in the history**: the Approach History has a **Landings | Departures** switch; the departures show the wind of each climb-out per height band and, in the WS view, their windshear report.
- **Departure ramps on trial**: the headwind-ramp analysis of the landings now also runs along each climb-out, as a trial without alerts (shown in italics in the departures' WS view) — they will alert only after the data has shown that they work. The Windshear user guide (2.0) and the study log (1.2) explain it.
- **Windshear page opens ready to watch the approaches**: on your first visit the ILS profile shows the wind barbs of the lowest aircraft (**Barbs**, **Hi**, **Auto**, **HW**), the **Approach History** is open and the **ACI** chart covers 12 hours. Whatever you change — barb buttons, Approach History open or closed, the chart view and range — is remembered in your browser for the next visit.
- **Windshear — turboprop departures**: the ATR 72's climb-out winds vary more than those of the jets (it accelerates, retracts its flaps and turns at 1 200–1 800 ft), so its departures got a band advisory five times as often. For turboprop departures the band limits are now 13 kt (advisory) and 19.5 kt (windshear alert) instead of 10 and 15 kt; jets and all landings are unchanged. About half as many false departure advisories. The Windshear user guide (1.9) and the windshear study log (1.1) explain it.
- **System health**: the **Calibrations** dot on the Statistics page now also turns amber when the magnetic declination model is missing on the server. The aircraft report their magnetic heading; the wind needs the true one, and without the model the winds far from the airport are a few knots less accurate. This happened unnoticed for a few hours on 27 October and is now shown at once (Statistics user guide 1.6).
- **Maintenance page — database snapshots**: a snapshot can now be written **compressed (gzip)**, about half the size, and the snapshots and monthly archive files have a **Download** button, so a copy of the database for analysis is a click away; old snapshots can be deleted there.
- **Behind the scenes**: MODE-S Wind can now also run on a cloud server with a secure **https://** address, fed live from the same receiver as the home station; it is updated from GitHub with one command, and the Radarcape receiver panel on the Statistics page works there too.

One picture at a time fills the screen: the **Skew-T** (now, or any stored hour), the **Time–height** section, the live **ILS approach** — the glideslope with the aircraft on final, their trails and the wind barbs of the lowest one (tap another aircraft or its strip to follow it) — or the **approach conditions and windshear** chart with the latest landings. Everything is chosen from the **☰** menu at the top left: tap a view's tile, and its options open below as tap buttons and switches; the other views' options are one tap away. Tap the diagram for the values. Use *Add to Home Screen* in the phone's browser to get a MODE-S Wind icon that opens it full screen like an app. The small **NEW · Mobile: /app** notice in the top bar is a reminder of the address (hover it for a short explanation).
- **Statistics page: 7 days** — a fifth **7 d** window button shows the whole last week in every table, the signal histogram and the range rings, and the message-rate chart has a **7 d** view (per 10 minutes, with the date) to compare the days. It uses the hourly records already saved, so the week fills up as the history grows (it began on 25 October). The Statistics user guide (version 1.4) is updated.
- **Less mobile data in the phone's ILS view**: it now loads only the aircraft on the approach and the alerts, compressed — about a tenth of before, roughly 1–4 MB for an hour of watching. The Windshear page's live data is compressed too.
- **Radarcape receiver** panel on the Statistics page (under the range rings): is the receiver's GPS working — antenna, satellites, the 1PPS pulse and the nanosecond timestamps that MLAT and the GPS quality monitor depend on — each shown as a green, amber or red chip (hover it for the meaning); the temperature of its GPS module with the lowest and highest of the day (the module runs about 20 °C warmer than the air around it, so this is not the outside temperature), how long the receiver has been running, where its clock takes the time from, its position and height, and whether its settings are the ones this system expects.
- The **Interrogators** list on the Statistics page now ends level with the Meteo yield panel; scroll inside it for the rest of the codes.
- A new **Radarcape** dot among the system health indicators turns amber or red when the receiver's GPS or timestamps have a problem, it runs hot, a setting has changed or its status cannot be read.
- The **range rings** are centred on the receiver's own GPS position while its GPS is working well; otherwise on the antenna position set in the configuration (or the map centre).
- The Statistics user guide (version 1.2) has a new section on the receiver panel.
- New user guide **MODE-S Wind on a phone** (in the *User guides* tab, and in the phone menu under *General*); the Skew-T and Sounding page guides show the new place of the MEPS wind barbs.
- **Live Map weather overlay, Both**: the model's rain is no longer hidden under the radar — inside the radar area it is drawn as outlines (thin from 0.1 mm/h, thick from 1 mm/h; white on the dark map, dark on the light one) over the radar's colours, so you can see where the forecast expected rain and where the radar actually sees it.
- **Skew-T with MEPS**: the model's wind barbs now stand beside the aircraft's in the right margin instead of over the diagram, so the two columns of barbs and the values are side by side and the curves stay clear. On a narrow Skew-T (a phone, or with **RH** on) the labels at the top left now continue on a second line instead of disappearing under the RH strip, and the temperature lines no longer run over the barbs and values.
- **MEPS check**: the model is now compared at the places where the aircraft actually flew, not as an average over the whole area. The aircraft heights now agree with the model within a few metres (before they looked 10–25 m too low on windy days), and the wind differences at the jet levels halved.
- **Windshear advisories**: a small headwind change (10–12 kt) on final is an advisory only when it stands out from what the other aircraft of the last 45 minutes met at the same heights — the normal increase of the wind with height on a calm evening is no longer flagged. Stronger changes and the windshear and microburst alerts work as before.
- The **system health** dots no longer flicker: the Raspberry Pi dot stays green until 72 °C, and quiet night-time traffic no longer turns the feed or decoding dot amber or red.
- **RH strip (trial) on the Live Map**: each layer now shows two bars — the upper, filled one is the hour in progress (it changes as new flights come in), the lower, outlined one the last full hour, which is steadier. Hover the strip for both values. Still an experiment.
- Weather page: the **−1h** / **+1h** buttons by the time slider now show their text centred.
- Windshear page: an aircraft on its third approach (after two go-arounds) is now marked **3x APP** instead of 2x.
- **When the MEPS model data runs out**: if newer model runs have not arrived (e.g. MET Norway's server is down), the Skew-T now says up to which hour the model data reaches and from which run, and the **MEPS** health dot turns amber instead of staying green.

## v.24102026 — New windshear detection; the old algorithms are retired; wind and gusts on the Weather page; upper-level temperatures corrected; MEPS in the Indices panel and in the Skew-T analysis

- **The six old windshear algorithms are retired.** Pairwise, Gradient, Energy, Rate, Baseline and Kinematic are no longer on the Windshear page, and the algorithm selector, the ON/OFF button and the F-factor gate are gone. A study of more than 3 000 recorded landings showed that in calm weather they mostly reacted to the normal increase of the wind with height — Energy, for example, would have warned on almost every approach. Their description is kept in the user guide *Windshear detection v1 — retired algorithms* (User guides tab), so older screenshots and notes can still be read.
- **New windshear detection (v2) runs on the server** for every aircraft on final, all the time — also when nobody has the page open. It follows each aircraft's own headwind along its path (true airspeed − ground speed, straight from the aircraft's replies) and looks for sudden changes over 0.4–4 km, and it compares each flight's wind at each height with all landings and departures of the last 45 minutes, which shows a front or gust front changing the wind between flights.
- **New alert names**, as at airports: **WS advisory (ADV)** from 10 kt, **Windshear alert (WSA)** from 15 kt and **Microburst alert (MBA)** from a 30 kt headwind loss. Choose from which level the banner, strip badge and ILS-profile band appear (default WSA, or *Banner off*).
- **The alert log keeps the last 24 hours** and is shown again when you reopen the page; hover an entry for heights, distance and the F-factor.
- **Departures count too**: every climb-out is compared with the landings and departures of the last 45 minutes — whatever runway they used — and becomes a reference for the next flights. Helsinki-Vantaa works in waves and often lands on one runway while departing from another, so during a departure wave there may be no comparable landing for half an hour; the departures now fill those gaps. Their alerts are marked *departure* (`dep` in the log). In the recorded data a departure on 22R showed the sudden wind change of 17 October ten minutes before the first landing did, and another one a 16 kt change during the frontal passage on the night of 24 October.
- **A windshear report for every landing** (and departure): the Approach History has a new **WS** view with the level, the strongest headwind loss and gain, the F-factor and the change against the earlier flights. Flights of the last weeks get their report automatically after the update.
- In the first two weeks of data (calm weather) the new detector gave no windshear alert from a single approach and advisories on about 1 % of landings — mostly real low-level wind changes such as the night inversion of 11 October. The comparison with earlier landings caught the sudden wind change on runway 22L on 17 October around 09:04 UTC. The alerts are experimental research output, not an operational warning.
- **Upper-level temperatures corrected**: the aircraft read about 0.7 K too warm at cruise levels (around FL300), found the same way against the weather model and the radiosondes. The Skew-T and the time–height section now take this off above about FL240, learnt every hour from the MEPS model comparison; the Skew-T says *upper T corr.* and the hover shows the raw value. Against the soundings the error at these levels fell from 1.3 to 1.0 K. Below FL180 nothing changes, and the checks keep the raw values.
- **Humidity (trial)**: the moisture indicator now learns its offsets from the MEPS model's humidity every hour as well as from the soundings. The check after the front showed only a weak signal below 5 000 ft and none above so far, so it stays a trial while more data is collected — for the humidity itself use the MEPS line in the RH strip.
- **User guides updated**: the Sounding and Wind Map guide (version 3.1) explains the upper-level temperature correction and the new columns of the MEPS and Sonde checks, what the humidity trial showed through the front, the MEPS column of the Indices panel and *Analysis of*; the Skew-T guide (version 4.5) the *upper T corr.* badge and hover and the analysis of the MEPS model or the sonde with its cloud layers.
- **Model data stays optional**: everything is still measured from the aircraft alone; the MEPS model is only used for comparison and to learn these two small calibrations, and without it the system keeps working with the last learnt values or the soundings.
- **MEPS in the Indices panel** (Sounding page): tick **MEPS** above the Skew-T and the panel shows the model's indices in their own column next to Mode S — with **Sonde** ticked as well, all three side by side. The model's humidity gives the indices the aircraft cannot (Showalter, K index, Total Totals, precipitable water), and its surface values come from the model itself. Values of the model or the sonde that differ clearly from Mode S are shaded; hover any value to see the difference. In *Area · now* the model column is a short forecast (*fcst*).
- **Skew-T analysis of the model or the sonde** (Live Map and Sounding page): the new **Analysis of** selector below the diagram runs the Analysis on the **MEPS** model or the **Sonde** instead of the aircraft (Mode S stays the default). The model and the sonde bring their own surface values and humidity, so the cloud base, CAPE and Lifted Index of *Full* come from them. A tag at the top left says which profile is analysed; if it is not available (box unticked, no data, no network) the analysis stays with Mode S and the tag says so. From *Stability* up, the model or sonde analysis also shades the **likely cloud layers** — where the relative humidity is 90 % or more (over ice in sub-zero air) — with their base and top, e.g. *CLOUD 3,800ft–7,800ft*.
- **Windshear engine status line**: under the Windshear Alert header a single line now shows that the detection is running on the server (green dot), how many aircraft it is analysing, how many earlier flights the comparison can use (fewer than 3 at night: *band check waiting*), how many landings and departures it has checked today with their levels, and the strongest value of the day. Hover it for the details.
- **Wind barbs right away**: the wind barbs on the Windshear page's ILS profile are now collected on the server. When you open the page, the aircraft already on final show their barbs from the whole approach so far, instead of starting empty.
- **More data for windshear research**: each landing now also stores the aircraft's own ADS-B ground speed, broadcast about twice a second, to test later whether it can make the windshear detection finer. The user guides now say that the interrogations come from Fintraffic's wide-area multilateration (WAM) network as well as radars.
- **New user guide: Windshear detection** (User guides tab, or the **?** in the *Windshear Alert* header of the Windshear page): how the new detection works and is calculated, the research it builds on, how to read the alerts, the log and the WS view of the Approach History, with the wind change of 17 October as a worked example.
- **Wind and gusts on the Weather page**: two new tick boxes show the MEPS model's wind at 10 m as wind barbs and its gusts as shading — from 25 kt through gale (34 kt) and storm (48 kt) to hurricane force. Hover the map for the wind direction and speed (kt and m/s) and the gust. After the update the last day and the forecast get the wind within about half an hour; older hours show the map without it.

## v.22102026 — MEPS check, What's new and user guides, geoid correction, honest moisture uncertainty

- **MEPS check** (Sounding page, new view): our aircraft profile compared every hour with the MEPS weather model (the model FMI uses; data from MET Norway) — temperature, wind and the heights of the pressure surfaces, level by level, also at night and between the soundings. It also shows how much of the difference to the Jokioinen radiosonde comes simply from the 100 km between Helsinki and Jokioinen.
