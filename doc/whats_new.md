# What's new in MODE-S Wind

Short notes for users of the web pages, newest first. Each version is one day of
development (v.DDMMYYYY). The full technical history is in CHANGELOG.md.

## v.30102026 — Departures and all traffic on the phone's approach map, thinner turbulence lines, clearer charts

- **Grey ATC symbols on the Windshear map** — in the ATC style the aircraft whose wind barbs the profile shows is no longer yellow (blue in Light): it is grey like the others, as on the Live Map. Its flight strip still shows which one it is.
- **Windshear charts no longer cover the alert title** — on the Windshear page the ACI, EDR and BL charts are now the same height as the WS chart, so they stay above the *Windshear alert* title on a 1920 × 1080 screen, and the chart no longer changes height by itself after switching view.
- **Go-around runway in the aircraft types** — pointing at a type's red **GA** badge now shows each go-around with its own runway, time and height, then the landing runway: `go-around RWY 22L 08:43 at 1,725 ft` / `landed RWY 22L 08:57`. Windshear guide 2.22.
- **Turbulence on the Windshear map** — the new **EDR** button in the map bar (off at the start, then 30 min or 1 h) colours the paths of the recent landings on the finals by their turbulence, as on the phone: thin lines, green smooth, yellow light, orange moderate, red severe, the roughest piece on top, and a short summary at the lower left. Windshear guide 2.21.
- **ATC labels on the phone's approach map** — the new option *Labels on the map: ATC* gives every aircraft the three-line label of the ATC view in a small font: callsign / level and selected level (`007↓020`) / speed and type (`14 A320`). Mobile guide 1.27.
- **Half-width charts on the Windshear page** — the new **½** button next to the chart range makes the ACI, WS, EDR, BL and ST charts half as wide, so more of the map (and the ATC view) is free; up to 12 hours of data. Click again for the full width. Windshear guide 2.20.
- **Selected altitude written the same everywhere** — the Windshear map's *Full* labels and the phone's *Selected altitude* now use the Live Map's form: `FL120-A030` descending from FL120 to 3 000 ft, `A023+A050` climbing to 5 000 ft, `FL390=FL390` level (before `A023+020`).
- **Receiving-station overview, page 7: the extended label** — the ATC-style map page now shows Hover with live traffic of this afternoon: an A321 turning towards the final at 25° of bank, its six-line label with airspeeds, heading, bank and tailwind (User guides: *The receiving station*, version 1.8).
- **Hover for an extended ATC label** — in the ATC style of the Live Map and the Windshear map, the new **Hover** button (off at the start) makes the label of the aircraft under the mouse grow into six lines, as on a controller's display: also the selected level, vertical rate and autopilot mode, true and indicated airspeed and Mach, magnetic heading and track, drift and bank angle, wind with head- and crosswind, temperature, distance from the airport, NACp and squawk — what the receiver has just heard, updated every 2 s. Windshear guide 2.18.
- **Windshear map remembers where you left it** — position and zoom are kept in your browser; the **⌂** button beside the zoom buttons brings it back to the airport.
- **Windshear detection v8 on trial** — a sharper version of the windshear detection now runs beside the current one for a week, without alerts: it also uses the ground speed the aircraft broadcast twice a second and the second airspeed register, so it gets about twice as many headwind values. Its result shows in the Approach History when you point at a flight (*v8 (shadow)*). Study log 1.7, Windshear guide 2.16.
- **Tidier Windshear alert header** — the *ⓘ v2* link to the old, retired windshear algorithms is gone (their description is still in the User guides); next to the **?** of the Windshear guide a **? EDR** now opens *EDR explained*.
- **Receiving-station overview, page 7** — a new page on the ATC-style map for readers with an air traffic control background: how a full label is read, with live traffic of 30 October (User guides: *The receiving station*, version 1.7).
- **Calmer GPS pages** — GPS Quality and GPS Episodes now use softer colours, as the Flights page: muted signal colours, a one-colour heatmap, outlined tags and lighter episode lines on the map.
- **Statistics chart opens at 24 h** — the *Message rate* and *Data transfer* chart now shows the last 24 hours by default (before 1 h).
- **ATC style on the Windshear map too** — the Windshear map's **ATC** button now gives the same radar-display look as the Live Map (it replaces the old ATC and Black backgrounds), with **Rings** for range rings, and a new **Hist** button adds history plots in every map style. Windshear user guide 2.14.
- **ATC style on the Live Map** — the new **Style** button switches the map to a radar-display look: no street map, the airport, the approach lines, the coastline and range rings, light and grey symbols and labels in three lines (callsign / level and selected level / speed and type) with a leader line, kept apart automatically. Range rings around the airport with the **Rings** button (off, every 5 or 10 NM). A research display, not for controlling traffic.
- **Live Map like a radar display** — the aircraft are open squares (as on the Windshear map) with small square history plots behind them instead of the round dots. **Hist** chooses how many plots (off, 5, 10, 20 — about 30 s to 2 min) and **Vec** shows speed vectors: where each aircraft will be in 30 s, 1 min or 2 min.
- **New guide: EDR explained** — what the turbulence number EDR is, how aviation and the Hong Kong Observatory use it, and how MODE-S Wind computes it from the aircraft's replies, with the first results at Helsinki-Vantaa (User guides tab).
- **Pages open in the same tab** — the navigation no longer opens every page in a tab of its own; Ctrl- or middle-click still gives a new tab.
- **Selected altitude in the Live Map list** — after the altitude, the altitude set on the autopilot: `FL120-A030` descending to 3 000 ft, `FL120-FL080` descending to FL080, `FL108+FL380` climbing to FL380, `FL390=FL390` level.
- **Live Map labels like the Windshear map** — the new **Lbl** button steps the labels: off, callsign, registration, ICAO24, type, callsign + altitude (A023 / FL061), + type, and full with the autopilot's selected altitude (A023+A050, FL061-FL070).
- **Boundary-layer temperature to 5 000 ft** — the temperatures of the climb-outs now reach 5 000 ft (before 3 000 ft) in the time–height section and the BL charts; the hours since yesterday evening are filled in at the update. At night few aircraft depart, so the upper part is often empty then.
- **Receiving-station overview updated** — page 5 now shows the phone's approach map with the turbulence (EDR) of the last hour (User guides: *The receiving station*, version 1.6).
- **Go-arounds in the aircraft types** — on the Windshear page's statistics a type with a go-around gets a red **GA** badge; point at it to see those landings.
- **GA on the Windshear map** — after a go-around the aircraft's label shows a flashing red **GA** until it turns onto its next approach or leaves the map as a climb-out.
- **Fix: the boundary-layer chart kept jumping wider** — on the Windshear page the BL chart widened for a moment when the last hour had both a low-level jet and an inversion; it now keeps its width.
- **Calmer Windshear map labels** — callsign and altitude in the same size; the selected aircraft's square is smaller and only lightly marked.
- **Label levels on the Windshear map** — the new **Lbl** button steps the labels: callsign · type · callsign + altitude · + type · full (also the autopilot's selected altitude, `A023+020`).
- **Windshear landings in the aircraft types** — on the Windshear page's statistics a type with a windshear report (ADV, WSA, MBA) in the period is coloured and gets a badge (e.g. `WSA` `ADV×5`); point at it to see those landings and what raised the alert.
- **Trails under Speed vectors** — in the phone's ILS approach options the *Trails* switch is now right under *Speed vectors*.
- **What's new at the top of the phone menu** — next to the *MODE-S Wind* title, with the red dot when there is something new (it is still in *General* too). From *What's new*, the **User guides** button next to the title opens the guides.
- **Aircraft type on the phone's approach map** — *Aircraft type on the map: On* shows the type after the callsign (`FIN456 AT75`); with it or the selected altitude on, the labels and squares are a little smaller so that everything fits.
- **Selected altitude on the phone** — with *Selected altitude: On* the ILS approach view shows after each altitude the altitude the pilots have set on the autopilot panel, when the aircraft sends it: `A023+020` is at 2 300 ft, climbing (+) or descending (−), with 2 000 ft set.
- **Lighter selection on the phone's approach map** — the selected aircraft now has a thin white frame of the same size as the other approach traffic, and its label is like the others.
- **Clearer charts on the Windshear page** — the buttons under the chart now show less at a time: **ACI** the approach conditions with the turbulence (EDR) below them, so the two can be compared; **WS** the windshear alone; **EDR** the turbulence above the windshear.
- **WS + EDR chart on the phone** — the ILS approach view now shows by default a chart of the turbulence and the windshear of every landing and departure under the profile or the map (*Chart under the view*: WS + EDR, WS + ACI or Off), for 3, 6 or 12 hours (*Chart range*).
- **Thinner turbulence lines on the phone's approach map** — the turbulence (EDR) colours no longer cover the aircraft and their labels, and the departures' turbulence is drawn only when *Departures on the map* is *Shown*.
- **Departures on the phone's approach map** — with *Departures: Shown* the ILS approach map now shows every climb-out within 15 NM, with its runway (`22R↑`); before, almost none appeared. *All tracked (15 NM)* likewise shows all traffic in the area, also aircraft that are climbing.

## v.29102026 — The boundary layer (low-level jet, inversion) — also in the Sounding page's time–height section, the windshear reports and on the Skew-T, turbulence in the Approach History, turbulence (EDR) of every landing and departure, METARs and pilot reports, altitudes as ATC sees them on the phone, speed vectors, more room for the strips, the WS + ACI chart in the ILS view, landed aircraft leave sooner, a new Flights page

- **The receiving station overview, page 6** — the A4 / A3 overview (User guides) has a new page on turbulence (EDR) and the boundary layer: what they are, how MODE-S Wind measures them from the aircraft's replies, the first results and where to find them.
- **Windshear page map, lighter labels** — the selected aircraft has a thin white frame of the same size as the other approach traffic, and the callsigns are no longer bold.
- **Flights page in calmer colours** — the list now looks like the Approach History on the Windshear page, and the charts and the map track use softer colours.
- **Live Map: landed aircraft leave sooner** — an aircraft that has landed at the airport fades out 15 s after its last new position and is gone after 30 s, instead of staying on the map for many minutes (as already on the Windshear page and the phone).
- **The Flights page redesigned**: the flights on the left and the chosen flight on the right — its track on the map with wind barbs, the wind and temperature against altitude, the charts along the flight and the observations; hover a chart or a row to see the point on the map. One search box finds a callsign, an aircraft address or a registration (OH-ATI), and Landings / Departures / Soundings pick those flights, with the runway, the approach conditions, turbulence and windshear report of each landing or departure. The temperature now shows for most flights (it was mostly empty).
- **Registration database in the system health**: the Statistics page has a new health dot, **Reg. DB**, telling whether the optional aircraft registration database (BaseStation.sqb) is in use — green with the number of aircraft in it, a grey ring when it is not installed. It turns green within 5 minutes of placing the file, without a restart.
- **The boundary layer** — research: hour by hour the wind from the ground to 5 000 ft near the airport, the **low-level jet** (a band of strong wind a few hundred metres up, mostly at night) and the **surface inversion** (air above warmer than at the ground, on calm clear nights). On the Windshear page the Wind Rose panel has a new **BL** button (with **Wind / T** for the temperature), on the phone *ACI · WS · ST* has *Boundary layer · wind* and *· temperature*, and the ILS approach view a line under the aircraft, e.g. *LLJ 1750 ft · 20 kt* and *surface INV +8 K*.
- **Turbulence (EDR) in the Approach History**: the Windshear page's Approach History has a new view, **EDR** — how turbulent each landing and departure was, coloured by the ICAO classes (light from 0.10, moderate from 0.20), with a filter to list only the bumpy ones. On the phone each History card shows an **EDR** chip, and *Show: Turbulence* lists the values. Research — an average over the approach.
- **The boundary layer at the time of a windshear report**: an alert that happened during a low-level jet or a surface inversion gets a small **LLJ** / **INV** tag, and its details tell what the boundary layer was like then — in the alert log, the Approach History and the phone's History cards.
- **Low-level jet and inversion on the Skew-T**: with the **Airport** profile on, the Skew-T marks the low-level jet (◆ with its speed) and the surface inversion (a red bracket from the ground to its top) — on the Live Map, the Sounding page and the phone.
- **Departures followed higher**: climb-outs are now recorded up to 5 000 ft, so most departures get a turbulence value too.
- **Theme: Dark, Light or Auto** — the theme button at the top of every page is now an icon next to the clock, as on the phone: moon Dark, sun Light, half circle **Auto**, which follows your computer's light or dark setting. Auto is the new default; a click changes it, and your choice is remembered.
- **The Windshear page map looks like a radar screen**: open squares in the colour of the wind source, and labels in a radar-label font with the callsign over the altitude as ATC shows it (**A023**, **FL061**) and an arrow when climbing or descending; the speed vectors are on (30 s) from the start.
- **Departures stay hidden on the phone's approach map** (when *Departures* is *Hidden*) for their whole climb — before, they could appear above 3 300 ft, or when they turned across another runway's final.
- The guides explain them: Windshear (version 2.9), the phone guide (version 1.20) and the Skew-T guide (version 4.9). The windshear **study log** (version 1.6) adds what the turbulence and the boundary layer showed: the windshear flags of October came in turbulent air, and were rare in the calm, stable hours.
- **The boundary layer in the time–height section** (Sounding page and phone): two new fields, *Boundary layer · wind* and *· temperature*, show the lowest 5 000 ft at the airport for up to 14 days — the low-level jet as ◆ with its speed, the surface inversion in red. The new *Boundary layer* tick box draws the jet and the inversion on any other field, e.g. the lapse rate. Explained in the Sounding page guide (version 3.6) and the phone guide (version 1.19).
- **Turbulence (EDR)** — research: MODE-S Wind now works out how turbulent the air was for every landing and departure, from the aircraft's own Mode S replies, as the international turbulence measure EDR (ICAO: light from 0.10, moderate from 0.20, severe from 0.45). On the Windshear page the Wind Rose panel has a new **EDR** button and on the phone *ACI · WS · ST* a new choice **ACI + EDR + WS**: the approach conditions, the turbulence and the windshear on one time axis. On the phone's ILS approach map, *Turbulence on the map* (Off by default, 30 min or 1 h) colours the paths of the latest landings and departures by their turbulence, with a summary line under the aircraft.
- **METARs and pilot reports**: MODE-S Wind now keeps every METAR of Helsinki-Vantaa, and the pilot reports (ARS, WXREP) and SIGMETs of the FMI aviation weather service. When a METAR reports windshear (**WS R22L**) or a pilot reports windshear, turbulence or an inversion near the airport, the *ACI + windshear* chart shows it — an amber bar for the METAR, a **P** for the pilot report — so you can compare it with what the landings and departures showed. The messages themselves: on the Windshear page **Apch Hist › Reports**, on the phone **History › METAR & reports**; *WS & near* keeps only the windshear METARs and the pilot reports near the airport, *WS archive* the same for every date stored (also years back once the history has been loaded).
- **Altitudes as a controller sees them** (ILS approach on the phone): the map, the strips and the selected aircraft show the altitude as on a radar label — **A023** is 2 300 ft above sea level on today's QNH, above 5 000 ft the flight level (**FL061**). The map's altitudes are now corrected for the air pressure, as the Windshear page's profile already was.
- **The approach map looks like a radar screen**: each aircraft is an open square in the colour of its wind source, the selected one framed in white, with just its callsign and altitude.
- **Speed vectors** (phone and Windshear page): as on a radar screen, a line from each aircraft to where it will be in 30 seconds, 1 or 2 minutes — the faster it flies, the longer the line. On the phone: menu › ILS approach options › *Speed vectors*; on the Windshear page the **Vec** button above the map. Off by default.
- **Departures on the phone's map if you want them**: menu › ILS approach options › *Departures on the map* — Hidden (default) or Shown, marked with their runway and an arrow (`22R↑`). On the Windshear page the map shows no departures.
- **A smaller dot for the chosen aircraft** on the phone's ILS profile — the same size as the others, marked by its ring.
- **Lighter labels on the phone's approach map**: the callsigns are no longer bold (only the selected aircraft's is), and in the light theme the label plates are see-through, so the map under them stays visible.
- **More room for the flight strips**: with *Show: Both* the profile is lower and with *Show: Map* the map is smaller, so three or four strips fit under them on a phone.
- **WS + ACI chart in the ILS approach view**: the menu has a new option *WS + ACI chart* (Off, 3 h, 6 h). Switched on, the approach conditions and windshear chart of the last hours is shown under the profile or the map, before the strips.
- **Landed aircraft leave sooner** (Windshear page and phone): an aircraft that has landed used to stay on the map and in the strips for about a minute and a half. Now it fades 15 seconds after its last position near the runway (**LANDED?**) and goes after 30 seconds.
- The *MODE-S Wind on a phone* guide (version 1.15) and the Windshear guide (version 2.4) explain them.

## v.28102026 — History, space–time, Statistics and an approach map on the phone; a better search; maps remember their view; Pi or Server; signal chart

- **MODE-S Wind on your phone (`/app`): History** — a new tile shows the landings or the departures of the last hour to a day, or of any date, as cards: time, flight, type, runway, the ACI and windshear level, and a strip with the headwind (or crosswind, or wind) in each height band. The runway is shown in a small frame, lined up from card to card. Tap a card for all its bands and its reports. The search field on top finds a callsign, registration or aircraft type, e.g. *FIN7*, *OH-LX* or *AT75*.
- **Space–time on the phone**: the *ACI · WS · ST* tile (until now *ACI & WS*) has a third choice, **Space–time** — the same diagram as on the Windshear page, showing how the wind changes move through the successive flights. Tap a cell for its flights.
- **ACI · WS · ST — the list under the chart follows your choice**: it now lists the flights of the chosen period (before, always the latest 15 landings) — with *ACI + windshear* the departures too, with *Space–time* the flights of the diagram, or of the cell you tap. 15 rows, **Show all** for the rest, and **Landings / Departures in History** open the History view with the same period.
- **Approach History search** on the Windshear page: the box now finds a callsign, registration, aircraft type or ICAO address anywhere in the text (before only the beginning of a registration).
- Lighter on mobile data: the landings list now comes compressed and, for the phone, without the details it does not show.
- **A map in the phone's ILS approach view**: the aircraft on the approach are now also shown on a map above the glideslope profile (beside it when the phone is turned), as on the Windshear page — a small square with callsign and altitude (easy to read in both themes), the ILS lines and the airport. Tap an aircraft on the map to see its wind barbs on the profile. In the menu, *Show* chooses Profile, Map or Both, and *Aircraft on the map* also the other aircraft near the airport. The map stays where you leave it; the house button takes you back to the airport.
- **Statistics on the phone**: a new **Statistics** tile in `/app` shows what the receiver hears — the message rate (and the data transfer), the downlink formats, the meteo yield and the range rings, for the last 24 hours (or 10 min, 1 h, 7 d). In the menu's *Statistics options* you choose which sections are shown: signal strength, Comm-B registers, ADS-B type codes, reply cadence, the Radarcape receiver, the interrogators and the system health can be switched on. Each panel's explanation is folded under *About this panel*; tap the range rings for the farthest aircraft in that direction.
- **The time in UTC on the phone**: all times in MODE-S Wind are UTC (two hours behind Finnish time in winter), so `/app` now shows the current UTC time at the top right.
- **Light, dark or automatic on the phone**: the theme button at the top right of `/app` has three settings — **Dark** (moon), **Light** (sun) and **Auto** (half circle), which follows your phone's own dark / light setting, also when it changes in the evening. Each tap steps to the next one. Auto is used until you choose one.
- **About** now also tells about the two sites: the cloud server that serves you, and the Raspberry Pi station at the receiver that keeps observing even without the internet.
- **About and all the user guides on the phone**: the menu's *General* section now has **About** (the same as on the full site) and **User guides** — every guide, e.g. the Windshear guide or the windshear study log, opens as PDF on the phone.
- The *MODE-S Wind on a phone* user guide (version 1.11) has the new views.
- **The login is remembered**: after you log in, the browser remembers it for 30 days (renewed while you use MODE-S Wind) — the phone no longer asks for the user name and password again after the screen has been off, and the computer not after the browser has been closed. A new password logs you in again once.
- **What's new on the phone**: these notes are now also in `/app` — menu › *General* › **What's new**. A small red dot on the ☰ button tells when there is something new to read.
- **User guides always up to date**: a guide opened from the *User guides* list (also on the phone) is now always its newest version — before, a phone could keep showing a copy it had saved earlier.
- **The receiving station overview** (in the *User guides* tab) has a fifth page: **MODE-S Wind on your phone** — how to open `/app` (with a QR code to scan), pictures of the Skew-T, the ILS approach, the approach conditions and windshear chart and the Statistics on a phone, and what each view shows.
- **Windshear alerts: the change must be at least 14 kt**: a windshear alert (WSA) from a headwind ramp now needs the headwind itself to change by at least 14 kt, as at Hong Kong. Before, a short, sharp change of only 11 kt over 400 m could become a WSA; it is now an advisory (ADV). Since 9 October this changes two of the three ramp alerts (both on 26 October). The Windshear user guide (version 2.1) and the study log (version 1.5) explain it.
- **Windshear alerts in the controllers' short form**: each alert now also reads like the messages that airport windshear systems give to the tower — e.g. **15A WSA 15K+ 2MF** is *"Runway one five arrival, windshear alert, one five knot gain, two mile final"*, and **22RD WSA 15K- 1400FT** a 15-kt loss on the runway 22R climb-out at 1400 ft. You see it in the alert banner, the alert log and the ILS profile on the Windshear page, and in the ILS approach view on the phone; point at it (or at the WSA badge) for the spoken form. ADV is our own *advisory* level. They are marked **research**: MODE-S Wind is a research system, not an operational windshear alert. The Windshear user guide (version 2.2) explains the message.
- **The receiving station overview**: its windshear page now has the 14-kt rule and the new alert message with its spoken form.
- **Windshear study log (version 1.4)**: a comparison with the Hong Kong Observatory's lidar windshear method — what their papers say, how our alerts would look with their 14-kt alert rule (two of the three windshear alerts since 9 October would have been advisories), a diagram of every ramp's size against its length, and how much of the time an alert is in force (rarely: about 0.3 % of the time for a windshear alert).
- **Windshear study log (version 1.3)**: the first night of the trial ramps along the climb-outs — a night-time low-level jet over Helsinki-Vantaa, which the departures climbed straight into, and what that means before the departure ramps may give alerts.
- **Live Map, Wind Map and Weather remember where you were**: the map now opens with the same zoom and position you left it at. The new **⌂** button under the + / − buttons takes you back to the receiver.
- **Statistics page — Pi or Server**: the last health dot shows the computer the page runs on. On the cloud server it is now called **Server** (it never showed the receiving station's Pi there); on the Raspberry Pi it is still **Pi**. The Statistics user guide (version 1.7) explains it.
- **Statistics page — signal strength chart**: the chart now spans only the levels the receiver actually reports (here about −28 to 0 dBFS instead of −50 to 0), so the bars fill the chart and the scale is readable. The Statistics user guide (version 1.8) has the new picture.

## v.27102026 — Windshear alerts confirmed or isolated; departures compared fairly

- **Statistics page: data transfer** — a new tab **Data transfer** beside *Message rate* shows how much data the server moves: what it receives from the receiver (the raw Beast stream, the aircraft list), what it sends to the users, and everything through its network connection — now, today, yesterday, the last 7 days and this month, with an estimate for the whole month, and as a chart per minute, hour or day. The Statistics user guide (version 1.5) explains it.
- **Windshear page — confirmed or isolated**: a shear layer or a front is met by every aircraft that passes it, a turbulent eddy in strong wind by just one. Each ramp alert now shows whether the other landings on the same runway within 15 minutes saw the same: **✓2** = seen by two of them, **iso** = by none (probably turbulence), **iso?** = the landings after it are not known yet. Shown in the alert log and the Approach History *WS* view (hover for the details); the alert level itself does not change.
- **Fairer comparison of departures**: the aircraft's airspeed reads a little differently on approach and in the climb — departures showed about 3 kt more headwind than the landings at the same height, which made many departures look like a windshear advisory with "more headwind". Departures are now compared with the other departures (and landings with landings), or the difference is removed first. Fewer false departure advisories.
- The Windshear user guide (version 1.8) explains both, with the turbulent southerly day of 26 October as an example.
- **New: the windshear study log** (in the *User guides* tab) — how the windshear detection has developed and why: from the six first algorithms to the Hong Kong Observatory's method and each change since, with the checks of the collected data. Written for meteorologists and scientists, and kept up to date as the study goes on.
- **The receiving station** overview (in the *User guides* tab): three A4 pages with photos of the antenna, receiver and workstation, how the aircraft's replies become wind and temperature, the calibrations and the pages — and now also a page on the windshear detection and the ongoing study, and one on the two sites: the station at the receiver and the new cloud server.
- **New address: https://mode-s-wind.oh2gax.org** — MODE-S Wind now runs on a cloud server with a secure address; on a phone use **https://mode-s-wind.oh2gax.org/app** (and *Add to Home Screen* again from there). Please update your bookmarks and log in there with your personal user name and password. The old address shows a notice with the new one, and **Open this page there** takes you to the same page.
- **Windshear — space–time diagram**: a new **ST** button beside WR / ACI / WS shows how the wind changes move through the successive landings and departures — height upwards, time to the right, blue where the headwind dropped compared with the flights before, red where it rose. A front shows as a coloured streak through the flights, turbulence as scattered single cells. Hover a cell for its flights.
- **Windshear — departures in the history**: the Approach History has a **Landings | Departures** switch; the departures show the wind of each climb-out per height band and, in the WS view, their windshear report.
- **Departure ramps on trial**: the headwind-ramp analysis of the landings now also runs along each climb-out, as a trial without alerts (shown in italics in the departures' WS view) — they will alert only after the data has shown that they work. The Windshear user guide (2.0) and the study log (1.2) explain it.
- **Windshear page opens ready to watch the approaches**: on your first visit the ILS profile shows the wind barbs of the lowest aircraft (**Barbs**, **Hi**, **Auto**, **HW**), the **Approach History** is open and the **ACI** chart covers 12 hours. Whatever you change — barb buttons, Approach History open or closed, the chart view and range — is remembered in your browser for the next visit.
- **Windshear — turboprop departures**: the ATR 72's climb-out winds vary more than those of the jets (it accelerates, retracts its flaps and turns at 1 200–1 800 ft), so its departures got a band advisory five times as often. For turboprop departures the band limits are now 13 kt (advisory) and 19.5 kt (windshear alert) instead of 10 and 15 kt; jets and all landings are unchanged. ## v.24102026 — New windshear detection; the old algorithms are retired; wind and gusts on the Weather page; upper-level temperatures corrected; MEPS in the Indices panel and in the Skew-T analysis

- **The six old windshear algorithms are retired.** Pairwise, Gradient, Energy, Rate, Baseline and Kinematic are no longer on the Windshear page, and the algorithm selector, the ON/OFF button and the F-factor gate are gone. A study of more than 3 000 recorded landings showed that in calm weather they mostly reacted to the normal increase of the wind with height — Energy, for example, would have warned on almost every approach. Their description is kept in the user guide *Windshear detection v1 — retired algorithms* (User guides tab), so older screenshots and notes can still be read.
- **New windshear detection (v2) runs on the server** for every aircraft on final, all the time — also when nobody has the page open. It follows each aircraft's own headwind along its path (true airspeed − ground speed, straight from the aircraft's replies) and looks for sudden changes over 0.4–4 km, and it compares each flight's wind at each height with all landings and departures of the last 45 minutes, which shows a front or gust front changing the wind between flights.

Helsinki-Vantaa works in waves and often lands on one runway while departing from another, so during a departure wave there may be no comparable landing for half an hour; the departures now fill those gaps. Their alerts are marked *departure* (`dep` in the log).
